Drumming Styles: A Distributional Feature Analysis

Maximilian Van Zandt | Department of Statistics, University of Virginia

2025-12-01

Introduction

Question: Can we quantify drumming style?

Problem: Quantify latent dimensions of drumming performance style using interpretable statistical methods.

Data: Groove MIDI Dataset (Gillick et al., 2019)

  • 446,312 hits
  • 10 drummers
  • 1,150 recordings

Hypotheses:

H₁: Variation in drumming style decomposes into independent latent dimensions spanning kit utilization (pitch), dynamics, and temporal structure.

H₂: Individual drummers exhibit distinct, quantifiable distributions in velocity, timing, and pitch.

Goal: Find interpretable dimensions of playing style.

Methods

Dimension Reduction
Principal Components Analysis on 31-dimensional feature space (5 velocity, 4 timing, 22 voice proportion features), z-score standardized.

Distributional Analysis
Kernel Density Estimation with Gaussian kernels, bandwidth h = 1.06σn−1/5 (Silverman).

Temporal Extension
3-state Hidden Markov Models on rock recordings, Baum-Welch EM estimation, Gaussian emissions.

Conclusion

What I found:

  1. Drumming style has three independent dimensions - not just “loud vs soft” or “fast vs slow”.

  2. Every drummer has a unique velocity fingerprint - KDE reveals three distinct patterns.

  3. Temporal structure matters - HMM shows sustained periods within dynamic states rather than rapid fluctuations, indicating deliberate choices.

Why it matters: Quantifiable, interpretable methods work. Can be applied to music pedagogy, style classification, and generative models.

Next: Test across genres, expand to larger dataset with audio features. Expand HMM analysis to more recordings, potentially exploring other state-space models for temporal transitions.
